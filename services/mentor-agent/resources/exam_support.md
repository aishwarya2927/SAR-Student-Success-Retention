Exam Support

Purpose

This resource explains SPIT's examination structure and re-examination pathways so mentors can correctly advise students who are flagged as at-risk with an upcoming internal or end-semester assessment.

Available Support

SPIT's End Semester Examination (ESE) is typically a 3-hour, 100-mark written paper (or a practical/oral/quiz format for lab-based courses). Students who are detained due to attendance can, in specific attendance bands, still access the regular or special examination pathway rather than losing the semester outright. A re-examination pathway exists for students who do not clear a course in the regular ESE: they must attend the online/offline summer term classes, maintain 75% attendance in that term, and appear for the re-examination, where a minimum score of 40/100 is required to pass (with an applicable grade penalty). A separate grade-improvement examination is available in the same academic year for students who scored between CC and DD grades in the regular ESE, also subject to a one-grade penalty.

Eligibility / Rules

Students named on a defaulter list (attendance or dues related) are barred from the regular examination. Students detained with attendance below 50% in the ongoing semester are permitted to appear in the Special Examination on their first attempt only; those who fail the Special Examination's first attempt reappear through the standard re-examination cycle. Students seeking exam accommodations (e.g., for a documented medical condition) must route this through the Dean of Academics' office in advance, consistent with the same relaxation process used for attendance.

Recommended Faculty Actions


For a flagged student with two or more backlogs and an exam within the next few weeks, prioritize helping them understand which examination pathway (regular, special, re-exam, or grade-improvement) actually applies to their specific case, since eligibility differs by attendance band and prior attempt history.
Advise students that the summer-term re-examination route requires maintaining 75% attendance in that term itself — it is not a guaranteed pass path.
Direct students with a documented medical condition to request exam accommodations through the Dean of Academics' office well before the exam date, since this is not processed automatically.
Clarify to students on the borderline of a defaulter list that dues or attendance defaults can bar ESE participation entirely, independent of academic preparedness.
