Tutoring

Purpose

This resource describes IIT Madras's peer-driven academic tutoring channel so mentors can direct students with a specific subject-level knowledge gap toward senior-student academic help.

Available Support

Through the SAATHI program's "Acad Buddies" initiative, senior students help freshers who are struggling with specific academic subjects, walking them through coursework difficulties in whichever course a student finds challenging. Beyond the free peer-guidance format, students can also avail paid tutoring classes through the same Acad Buddies channel for more structured, ongoing subject support. This sits alongside SAATHI's broader Student Mentorship Cell, which covers day-to-day adjustment questions (courses, professors, institute life) more generally, meaning a student can approach either a general mentor or specifically request an Acad Buddy for subject-level help.

Eligibility / Rules

Acad Buddies support is aimed primarily at first-year students navigating a challenging academic environment, though the underlying Student Mentorship Cell relationship (which a student can approach for "anything under the roof," including academic doubts) is broader and ongoing. There is no formal at-risk flag required to request an Acad Buddy; a student can reach out directly through SAATHI's channels. Because Acad Buddies are senior-student volunteers, availability for a specific subject depends on which seniors have opted in for that term, and the paid-tutoring option is a separate, explicit arrangement rather than the default free peer support.

Recommended Faculty Actions


For a student with a clear, subject-specific knowledge gap (rather than a broader disengagement pattern), recommend the Acad Buddies channel as the first, low-barrier option before recommending paid or external tutoring.
Clarify to the student upfront which format they are entering — free peer guidance versus the paid tutoring option — since both exist under the same program.
Encourage students to also stay connected to their general Student Mentorship Cell mentor, since that relationship can help identify when a subject-specific gap is part of a broader adjustment issue.
Where a subject-specific Acad Buddy isn't currently available, suggest the general mentor as an interim point of contact to avoid the student feeling unsupported while a subject match is arranged.
