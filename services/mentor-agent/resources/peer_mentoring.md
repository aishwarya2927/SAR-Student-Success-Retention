Peer Mentoring

Purpose

This resource describes IIT Madras's student-led peer mentoring structures so mentors can distinguish disengagement-driven risk (best served by peer connection) from purely academic or financial risk factors.

Available Support

IIT Madras's SAATHI program is a proactive, student-led initiative focused on self-growth and wellbeing that runs a Student Mentorship Cell alongside "Acad Buddies" and "Hobby Buddies." Acad Buddies are senior students who help freshers with academic subjects they find difficult, and students can also access paid tutoring classes through this same program. Hobby Buddies connect students with shared interests across branch, language, and region, specifically to build informal social connections. Separately, MITR is a retroactive, student-and-faculty peer-support body — distinct from academic mentoring — operating under the motto that no one on campus should be unattended in their emotional distress, with presence across hostels and departments.

Eligibility / Rules

Acad Buddies and Hobby Buddies are informal, opt-in programs coordinated through SAATHI and are open to students seeking academic or social support; there is no formal at-risk flag required to access them. MITR peer-support volunteers are trained in basic counselling and empathy skills and can be approached directly or through hostel/department presence; MITR is oriented toward emotional distress rather than academic content. Because both programs are student-run, availability of a specific Acad Buddy for a given subject depends on senior-student volunteer coverage in that term.

Recommended Faculty Actions


For students flagged as at-risk primarily due to disengagement (low LMS activity, low social/extracurricular involvement) rather than a single academic or financial factor, recommend an Acad Buddy or Hobby Buddy connection over formal tutoring, since the underlying issue is more likely isolation than a knowledge gap.
For a subject-specific academic gap, recommend the Acad Buddies channel specifically, noting that some Acad Buddy support is offered as paid tutoring rather than free peer help.
If distress signals accompany disengagement (not just academic disengagement), route the student toward MITR in parallel, since MITR is built for early, informal peer support before a situation escalates to needing the Wellness Centre.
Since both SAATHI and MITR are entirely student-run, encourage warm hand-offs (e.g., introducing the student to a specific mentor or hostel-level MITR volunteer) rather than only sharing a generic contact.
