The Peer Mentoring Program pairs first- and second-year students with trained third- and fourth-year volunteer mentors from the same branch, on a roughly one-mentor-to-five-mentee basis, coordinated through the Student Affairs Office. Unlike faculty mentoring, which focuses on academic and disciplinary oversight, peer mentors focus on day-to-day adjustment: navigating coursework difficulty, time management, choosing electives, internship and placement preparation, and general college-life questions a student may be hesitant to raise with faculty. Peer mentor groups meet informally at least once every two weeks, with most communication happening over a dedicated WhatsApp or Discord group per pairing. Students who are flagged as at-risk primarily due to disengagement (low LMS activity, low extracurricular involvement) rather than a single academic or financial factor are often better served by an active peer-mentor connection than by formal tutoring, since the underlying issue is frequently a sense of isolation rather than a knowledge gap. New peer-mentor pairings are formed at the start of each semester, but a student can request a peer mentor mid-semester at any time by contacting the Student Affairs Office directly.
