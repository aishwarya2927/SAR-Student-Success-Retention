Counselling

Purpose

This resource describes IIT Bombay's Student Wellness Centre (SWC) so mentors can direct students showing academic stress, anxiety, or adjustment difficulty toward appropriate, confidential mental health support.

Available Support

The Student Wellness Centre (SWC) offers one-on-one counselling, group therapy, mindfulness workshops, peer support, and wellness programs delivered by qualified mental health professionals, with in-person appointments available Monday–Friday, 10:30 AM to 5:30 PM, each session typically lasting 45 minutes. Students who prefer not to meet in person can use free online counselling through TalktoAngel, a platform SWC has partnered with, offering 24x7 confidential audio/video sessions in multiple Indian languages, accessible by signing in with an institute email ID. For students who want care outside the institute, SWC also coordinates an External Counselling referral system connecting students to partner hospitals and counselling centers in the city, with the institute providing reimbursement for these consultations.

Eligibility / Rules

Any IIT Bombay student can self-refer for a first counselling appointment through the SWC website, and intake appointments are scheduled at times convenient to the student's academic commitments; where more precise diagnosis is needed, SWC also conducts psychological assessments administered by a trained psychometrician. Online counselling via TalktoAngel requires sign-up and OTP verification using the student's registered IITB email ID to access free-of-charge sessions. The External Counselling portal is only accessible from within the IITB network, and students should read the referral sheet's terms before booking an external appointment.

Recommended Faculty Actions


For a student showing high stress indicators alongside academic risk factors, recommend an SWC appointment and mention the option of the faster, always-available TalktoAngel online channel if in-person scheduling is a barrier.
Make clear to the student that a faculty mentor can refer them to SWC only with the student's awareness and consent — never as an unannounced report.
For students hesitant about in-person, on-campus counselling, the TalktoAngel option may lower the barrier to a first conversation, since it can be done privately and does not require visiting the SWC in person.
If a student is in acute distress, note that SWC prioritizes emergency support for students in crisis, and this should be communicated as an active recommendation rather than only one option among several.
